Cover Lesson: The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Cambridge IGCSE Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 · Physics P3.3.4 & P3.3.5 · Approx. 60 minutes · No specialist knowledge needed: everything you need is on this page.

Learning objectives
  • Name the 7 regions of the EM spectrum in order of frequency and of wavelength.
  • Know all EM waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum: 3.0 × 108 m/s (about the same in air).
  • Give applications of each region.
  • Describe the harmful effects of UV, X-rays and gamma rays.
You need: textbooks and/or student devices, A4 paper (one sheet per group), marker pens, whiteboard, Blu-Tack/magnets.

Lesson plan

1. Starter: which is faster, light or sound? (10 min)

Write the question on the board. Take answers, then ask for evidence from real life.

“Which travels faster, light or sound? Can you give an example where you can see this?”

Expected: lightning then thunder, fireworks then bang, starting pistol smoke then sound. Point out light is roughly a million times faster than sound (3.0 × 108 m/s versus about 340 m/s).

2. Introduction (10 min)

Explain, and have learners copy into their books:

  • The electromagnetic (EM) spectrum is a family of waves with some properties in common and some different.
  • Same: all travel at 3.0 × 108 m/s in a vacuum (approximately the same in air), and all can travel through a vacuum (that is how sunlight reaches us).
  • Different: wavelength, frequency, uses, and how they affect us.
  • Draw the coloured bar above on the board, labelling the two arrows (wavelength decreases, frequency increases).

3. Group research and presentation (25 min)

  1. Groups of 3–4. Assign one region per group (7 regions; some groups can double up, or give the quicker group two).
  2. Using textbooks or devices, each group writes on one sheet of A4, in large marker: region name, one or more applications, and any harm caused (leave blank if none in the syllabus).
  3. Allow about 12 minutes research, then each group gives a 30-second presentation.
  4. After presenting, the group sticks their sheet on the board in the correct order along the wavelength/frequency arrows. The class checks the order using the fact sheet.

Behaviour tip: give each group roles (writer, reader, presenter, timekeeper).

4. Plenary and practice questions (15 min)

Recap the key points from the board. Then learners answer in silence (answers below). Optional: play the “Electromagnetic Spectrum” song by Emerson and Wong Yann, and explore scaleofuniverse.com to compare wavelengths with everyday objects.

  1. List the seven regions in order of increasing frequency.
  2. Which region has the longest wavelength? Which has the highest frequency?
  3. State the speed of all EM waves in a vacuum.
  4. Name one use of microwaves and one use of infrared.
  5. Give one use of X-rays.
  6. Why is too much UV exposure dangerous?
  7. Why are X-rays and gamma rays dangerous to living cells?
  8. Which travels faster in air: a gamma ray or a radio wave?
Answers (click to reveal)
  1. Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
  2. Radio (longest wavelength); gamma (highest frequency).
  3. 3.0 × 108 m/s.
  4. Microwaves: satellite TV, mobile phones or microwave ovens. Infrared: TV remote controls or thermal imaging.
  5. Medical scanning or security scanners.
  6. It damages surface cells and the eyes, which can lead to skin cancer and eye conditions.
  7. They can damage cells or cause mutations in the body.
  8. Neither: they travel at the same speed (approximately 3.0 × 108 m/s).

Support, extension and notes

  • Support: give learners the fact sheet table with the “Applications” column blank to fill in; allow the colour bar to be copied.
  • Extension: “Why do we use X-rays for bones but not for routine check-ups?” Or explain why a microwave oven door has a metal mesh. Link to the next topic: calculating wave speed with v = fλ.
  • Common mistake: learners may think radio waves are sound waves. Radio waves are EM waves that travel at the speed of light; sound is a different kind of wave and needs a medium.
  • If time runs short: skip the song and website, and shorten presentations to one sentence.
  • Please leave: a list of any learners who were absent or off task, and the group sheets for the class teacher.
