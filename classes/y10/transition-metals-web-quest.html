Transition metals web quest

Iron, copper, nickel, titanium and their neighbours sit in the middle block of the periodic table. Your job is to find out what they have in common, and how they differ from metals such as sodium and magnesium. Use the websites suggested in each mission, then check your answers.

Mission 3: More than one ion

Sodium chloride is always NaCl. Magnesium chloride is always MgCl₂. Now look at iron and copper.

Where to look:
  • Search for iron(II) chloride formula and iron(III) chloride formula.
  • Search for copper(I) oxide and copper(II) oxide.

Write what this tells you about the ions that iron and copper can form. Explain what the Roman numerals in the names mean.

Model answer: Iron forms more than one ion (Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺), and copper forms Cu⁺ and Cu²⁺. Transition metals can have variable oxidation states. The Roman numeral gives the charge on the metal ion, so iron(III) chloride contains Fe³⁺ and its formula is FeCl₃. Group I and II metals form only one ion, so they do not need a numeral.

Exam practice

State three properties that are typical of transition elements but not of Group I metals.

Any three from: high melting points; high densities; form coloured compounds; act as catalysts; form ions with different charges (variable oxidation states); form hard, strong metals.
