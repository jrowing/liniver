Stage 1 12 minutes

What is a sexually transmitted infection?

Objective: define an STI and state that HIV is a pathogen that causes one (B15.5.1, B15.5.2).

A sexually transmitted infection (STI) is an infection that is transmitted through sexual contact.

STIs are caused by pathogens – microorganisms that cause disease. The pathogen is passed from one person to another in body fluids such as semen, vaginal fluid and blood, or by contact between the moist linings of the reproductive organs.

HIV stands for human immunodeficiency virus. HIV is a pathogen – a virus – that causes an STI.

Other STIs are caused by bacteria, for example chlamydia, gonorrhoea and syphilis. Bacterial STIs can be treated with antibiotics. Antibiotics do not work against viruses such as HIV.

Many STIs cause no symptoms at first, so a person can be infected, and pass the infection on, without knowing. This is one reason testing matters.

Stage 2 20 minutes

How HIV infection may lead to AIDS

Objective: state that HIV infection may lead to AIDS (B15.5.3).

HIV infects and destroys lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell. Lymphocytes make antibodies and are central to the immune system.

Inside a lymphocyte, HIV makes copies of itself. New virus particles are released and infect more lymphocytes. Over several years the number of lymphocytes falls.

When there are too few lymphocytes, the body can no longer fight off other pathogens. The person catches infections a healthy immune system would normally defeat, such as tuberculosis and pneumonia. This condition is AIDS: acquired immune deficiency syndrome.

HIV infection may lead to AIDS.

Why may? Without treatment, most people with HIV eventually develop AIDS. Antiretroviral drugs stop the virus from multiplying. They do not cure HIV, but a person who takes them every day can keep a healthy number of lymphocytes, never develop AIDS, and live a long life.

People with AIDS do not usually die from HIV itself, but from the other infections their immune system can no longer fight.

Stage 3 15 minutes

How HIV is transmitted

Objective: describe the methods of transmission of HIV (B15.5.4).

HIV is carried in certain body fluids: blood, semen, vaginal fluid and breast milk. It is passed on when one of these fluids from an infected person gets into another person's bloodstream or touches the moist linings of their body.

HIV does not survive long outside the body, and it is not spread through saliva, sweat, tears, air, water or insects.

MethodHow the virus passes
Unprotected sexual intercourseSemen or vaginal fluid carrying HIV contacts the linings of the vagina, anus or penis
Sharing needlesBlood left in a needle or syringe used by an infected person enters the next user's blood, e.g. when injecting drugs
Transfusion of infected bloodBlood from an infected donor is put directly into the bloodstream; now rare where blood is screened
Mother to babyAcross the placenta during pregnancy, through blood during birth, or in breast milk

Exam-style practice

Explain how the spread of HIV can be controlled. [4]

Mark scheme (open after you've written your answer)

Any four of these, 1 mark each. Each method needs its reason.

  • use condoms; barrier stops body fluids passing between partners
  • fewer sexual partners; fewer people the virus could pass to or from
  • testing and contact tracing; infected people and their partners found, treated and can avoid passing it on
  • antiretroviral treatment; lowers amount of virus in body fluids so less likely to be transmitted
  • do not share needles / use needle exchange; no infected blood passed between users
  • screen donated blood; infected blood not used in transfusions
  • treatment for HIV-positive mothers / avoid breastfeeding where safe alternatives exist; reduces transfer to the baby
  • education; people know the risks and how to protect themselves

A method with no explanation scores nothing. Check your answer against the list and mark it out of 4.
