Mutual induction between two coils

A changing current in the primary coil makes its magnetic field grow, collapse and reverse. Wherever that changing field links the secondary coil, an emf is induced and a current flows in the second circuit. Dots show charge moving in each circuit.

Primary current (field strength) Emf induced in secondary

With AC, the induced emf is largest as the primary current passes through zero, where it changes fastest, and zero at the current’s peaks, where it is momentarily steady.

With the battery, the meter kicks one way when the switch closes and the other way when it opens, and reads zero while the current is steady. Opening the switch collapses the field faster, so the kick is bigger.

Take the iron core out and less of the primary’s flux links the secondary, so the emf drops. More secondary turns give a bigger emf.

Slowed down for viewing: mains alternates at 50 Hz. The primary current is drawn in step with the supply for simplicity.
